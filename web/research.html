How the record is built

Observe. Model. Retain.

Each layer is kept distinct so an observation, a simulated decision, and a later outcome can be examined in context.

01

Observe public market conditions

We record time-stamped price and market-cap bars, liquidity, volume, buy/sell activity, token identifiers, and available source metadata.

02

Apply declared rules

Each strategy uses a published configuration for its observation window, entry conditions, risk limits, modeled costs, and exits.

03

Save evidence over time

Lifecycle markers and scheduled forward outcomes are retained with timing and quality fields, including late or missed observations.

Transparency

What the research can support.

The public record is designed for checking methods and limitations—not for presenting simulation output as a trade recommendation.

Modeled execution

Fills, costs, and position marks are simulations. They are not on-chain orders or proof that a live trade could fill at the same price.

Forward, not backfilled

Once an instance begins, it records what follows. New versions run in separate ledgers rather than rewriting an older result.

Coverage is visible

Late and missed outcome observations are retained as quality information; they are not silently counted as successful coverage.

Downloadable evidence

Public JSONL archives contain the normalized market research and execution records behind this view. The current snapshot reports freshness.

Execution conditions we model

  • Observed bonding-curve or indexed AMM reserve quotes
  • Configured swap fees, fixed network costs, and adverse-fill allowance
  • Signal expiry, entry / exit latency, and fresh-quote checks
  • Failed attempts and unavailable exits that remain visible in the ledger
We do not recreate mempool ordering, validator behavior, priority-fee auctions, routing, or every live liquidity condition. Parameters are published with each agent configuration.

Forward measurement

Outcome-window coverage

Scheduled observations test how outcomes develop after a modeled event. On-time coverage allows a delay of 10% of the horizon, with a minimum of 60 seconds and maximum of one hour.

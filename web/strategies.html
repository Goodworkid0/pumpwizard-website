How the live-market simulation worksPublic inputs · modeled execution · recorded evidence⌄

The engine observes public Pump.fun curve data and, after graduation, available indexed PumpSwap market data. It tests whether a defined signal can survive real-time market conditions after modeled costs and delays—not whether a chart can be made to look good in hindsight.

1. Observe & validate

We accept only fresh, structurally valid quotes. The route, pool, market cap, price, reserves, liquidity, timestamps and strategy-specific age limits must agree. Stale, missing or mismatched data is rejected.

2. Screen a rule

Every strategy has a cap band, observation window, sizing and signal family. Rules look for patterns such as persistent growth, higher lows, breakouts, retests, liquidity growth or relative strength; a signal is a condition to test, not a price prediction.

3. Model the fill

Signals become paper orders only after a later usable quote and configured delay. Size is calculated from the observed curve or indexed AMM reserves, then reduced for modeled adverse execution and charged that instance’s fees, fixed costs and failed-attempt stress.

4. Mark, exit & compare

Later usable quotes value open positions. Strategies apply their configured stop, holding limit, targets and trailing rules; V2 trials also test an earlier net-value profit trail. A missing usable exit quote marks the unsold position at $0 rather than inventing a price.

Risk controls: entry liquidity and round-trip-loss screens, reserve-size limits, pool continuity checks, delayed fills, quote-gap handling, and—where the version requires it—fresh matching ownership evidence.

Audit trail: inputs, decisions, fills, sales, equity marks and immutable configurations are written to each strategy’s independent ledger. V1/V2 versions begin together on the same live feed so the fresh V1 control, not an older all-time history, is the comparison baseline.

Important: no wallet is connected and no order is sent. Public feeds can be delayed or incomplete, modeled prices are not executable quotes, and simulation results are not investment advice or a promise of returns. Read the full methodology and limitations →
